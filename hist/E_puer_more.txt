@puer|Tea from Yunnan's mountains is first recorded in the Tang dynasty, when Fan Chuo's Book of the Man noted tea "from the mountains around Yinsheng". The late-Ming Dian Lüe gives the earliest mention of "Pu tea", steamed and pressed into balls. Pu'er was never a growing area itself but the town where the tea was gathered and traded. In 1729 the Qing set up Pu'er Prefecture over the Six Ancient Tea Mountains east of the Mekong (Youle, Gedeng, Yibang, Mangzhi, Manzhuan and Mansa), and from then Pu'er was sent to the capital as a regulated tribute tea along the Tea Horse Road. In the Republican era Menghai replaced Pu'er as the trading hub and the "six mountains beyond the river" (Nannuo, Nanqiao, Mengsong, Jingmai, Bulang and Bada) rose in turn. Today tea is named after the mountain and village whose old trees it comes from.
易武|In the Qing, Yiwu (on old Mansa mountain) was the main gathering point of the Six Tea Mountains: about seven-tenths of their raw tea passed through it on the way to Pu'er Prefecture.
攸乐|After the Yongzheng reforms the Qing stationed a sub-prefect at Youle with 500 troops to keep order on the tea mountains.
