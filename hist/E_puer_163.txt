易武|Ancient Pu people are said to have planted tea at Yiwu more than a thousand years ago. In the late Qing, some of the most famous tea houses in Pu'er history were founded here, and Yiwu took over the trade that had once centred on nearby Manzhuan and Mansa.
曼撒|Mansa (慢撒) was once a large trading centre for tea in the Yiwu area, before the trade shifted to Yiwu itself.
倚邦|Yibang's tea area has a long history and still keeps a number of tea trees more than 500 years old. In the Qing dynasty, Mansong tea from Yibang became a tribute tea.
蛮砖|Manzhuan was already well known in the Qing dynasty. The Bencao Shiyi and the Dianhai Yuheng Zhi both praised the teas of Yibang and Manzhuan as tasting best. In the late Qing and early Republic, much of its tea was sold to the tea houses of Yiwu.
革登|The Pu'er Prefecture Gazetteer (普洱府志) records an exceptionally large "tea king" tree at Gedeng, which no longer survives.
莽枝|Minority peoples were living and growing tea at Mangzhi a thousand years ago, and there were whole tea gardens by the Yuan dynasty at the latest. Its village of Niugun Pond was the trading hub of the northern tea mountains in the early Qing.
牛滚塘|Niugun Pond was the trading hub of the northern Six Tea Mountains in the early Qing. A clash there with the local chieftain in the Yongzheng reign, the "Niugun Pond incident", was one of the events that led to the founding of Pu'er Prefecture.
攸乐|Jino Mountain (Youle) has grown tea for a very long time; Jino legend traces the people to soldiers of Zhuge Liang's army. Youle produced a large crop in the Qing, and the well-known "Ke Yi Xing" (可以兴) brick tea was made from Youle leaf.
曼糯|Mannuo was historically an important Pu'er tea area on the road towards Lancang. Many of its old tea trees were cut down to plant grain in the 1950s and 1960s.
勐宋|Mengsong is one of the oldest tea areas in Menghai. Han Chinese settlers moved in more than a century ago to trade in tea.
半坡寨|Legend says Zhuge Liang planted tea on Nannuo Mountain during his southern campaign. The tea king tree at Banpo village is cited as evidence of how long tea has been cultivated here. In the Republican era Nannuo Mountain was where machine tea-making in Yunnan began.
帕沙|Ancestors of the Hani people were already living and growing tea at Pasha village in the Tang and Song dynasties.
贺开|The Lahu of Hekai descend from the ancient Qiang and took up tea growing here very early; locals revere Zhuge Liang as their tea ancestor. Some cultivated trees in the area are close to a thousand years old.
布朗山|Pu'er tea historians trace tea growing by the ancient Pu people, ancestors of the Bulang, back to the Shang and Zhou periods. The old trees of Lao Banzhang on Bulang Mountain only became widely known in the early 2000s.
景洪勐宋|In the Qing dynasty, tea from Mengsong in Jinghong was presented to the local chieftains. Very old trees survive in the gardens near the old village of Manqian.
巴达|A wild giant tea tree was discovered at Bada in 1962.
章朗|Zhanglang, the old Bulang village of Bada, is more than 1,400 years old.
大雪山|Most of the wild tea trees on Daxueshan are over a thousand years old, and the mountain is home to a "world wild tea king".
懂过|Donguo has grown tea for a long time, but it was so remote that cars could not reach it until 2007.
香竹箐|The great tea tree at Xiangzhuqing is estimated at about 3,200 years old. Fengqing county also produced the historic Taihua tea, and its Fengqing Tea Factory once set China's standards for black tea.
鲁史|Lushi has been a key town on the Tea Horse Road for several hundred years.
忙肺|Most of Mangfei's tea trees are about 80 years old.
湾甸|Wandian tea from Zhenkang was the most celebrated tea of the area in the Ming dynasty.
须立贡茶|Xuligong tea was an imperial tribute tea in the Qing dynasty. Its town of Lianzhu was a well-known post on the Tea Horse Road.
迷帝贡茶|Tea was being grown at Midi by the Wanli reign of the Ming. It grew further in the Qing, when the village became a post on the Tea Horse Road; the name "Midi" (charming the emperor) is said to come from the emperor's fondness for it as a tribute tea.
景星|Jingxing has grown tea for several centuries, and had its own Jingxing Tea Factory before 1949, the forerunner of the Mojiang Tea Factory.
困鹿山|Kunlushan was one of the tribute tea gardens of the Qing dynasty.
宽宏|The Hani people of Kuanhong village on Kunlushan have grown tea for more than 400 years.
江城国庆|Jiangcheng lay on the old route north from Yiwu, and Luojie and Tianfang in Guoqing township have grown tea for more than 200 years.
络捷|Luojie in Jiangcheng has grown tea for more than 200 years.
田房|Tianfang in Jiangcheng has grown tea for more than 200 years.
老仓福德|In 1925 Yunnan's governor Tang Jiyao awarded Laocang tea a first-class prize.
金鼎|Jinding still has close to a thousand mu of tea trees more than a century old.
御笔|Yubi tea mountain still has close to a thousand mu of tea trees more than a century old.
哀牢山西坡|The western slope of the Ailao Mountains was historically an important route for Pu'er tea travelling north to Dali.
振太|Tea has been grown in the old tea area of Zhentai for more than 250 years.
老乌山|The Yi people of Laowushan have grown tea for more than 500 years.
田坝|Tianba still has several hundred mu of tea trees more than a century old.
马邓|Tea planting on Madeng began in the Jiaqing reign of the Qing, and Madeng tea is a traditional famous tea of Zhenyuan.
秧塔|Yangta white tea is one of the few Pu'er tribute teas with a clear record in historical texts.
邦崴|The Lahu around Bangwei have grown tea for several hundred years. Its "transitional-type" tea king tree was confirmed at Chinese and international symposia in 1992 and 1993.
文东|The Wa people of Xiaozhai in Wendong have grown tea for more than 500 years, and the Han people of Pasai for nearly 500.
芒中|Mangzhong's old tea garden was the private garden of the Dai chieftains of Menglian, whose pacification office stood in nearby Nayun.
千家寨|The 2,700-year-old wild tea king of Qianjiazhai stands in the Ailao Mountains of Zhenyuan.
