贡眉|Gongmei comes from Jianyang, and is generally held to have grown out of Nankeng's white Shoumei. How it got its name is not settled: one local story says the Shoumei made by the Xiao brothers in the Qing was bought by the court and became a Jianyang tribute tea, while others point to Jianyang's nearness to the Song tribute gardens of Beiyuan. Zhang Tianfu recorded that Shoumei was made in 1953–1954, stopped, and was revived in 1959 for export. The national standard defines Gongmei as made from shoots of the old seed-grown "caicha" bushes.

安化天尖|Anhua's dark tea is first recorded in the Tang. In 1391 the Ming court fixed an annual tribute of 22 jin, and in 1595 it was made "official tea" for the tea-horse trade. Its making craft was added to Anhua's county intangible heritage list in May 2014.
