@wuyi|Wuyi's named bushes (名丛) were picked out of the mountain's mixed local seedling stock, caicha (菜茶), as single plants with outstanding character. In 1942 the tea scholar Lin Fuquan surveyed the mountain and concluded there were no fewer than a thousand named bushes and varieties, over 830 around Huiyuan alone; his 1943 book recorded them. Many have since been lost. A named-bush garden was set up at Jiulong in 1980, and from 1990 Luo Shengcai collected 1,178 samples and propagated 112 named bushes, of which 86 survived. Rougui, itself once a named bush, was promoted as a cultivar from the early 1980s and now covers about a third of Wuyi rock tea land.

@wuyihist|These are "flower names" (花名) from Wuyi's historic registers: names given to single bushes or small groups picked out of the local caicha stock for their individual character. They were never produced in quantity and are not cultivars in the modern sense. More than eight hundred such names are recorded. In 1942 Lin Fuquan surveyed Wuyi's teas and listed about 280 rock tea flower names from the Huiyuan area alone in his 1943 book on Wuyi tea production; many of the bushes have since disappeared.
